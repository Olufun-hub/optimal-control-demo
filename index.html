Making the Best Decisions Over Time

Deterministic control, stochastic control and reinforcement learning — plug in numbers and watch the optimal decision change. QMB 6875.

The big picture

The whole topic in one sentence
Optimal control is optimization where the thing you choose is a plan over time, and every decision changes the situation for the next one.

The Bellman equation — the formula behind every tab
$$V_t(x)=\max_{u}\Big[\underbrace{R(x,u)}_{\text{payoff today}}+\delta\,\underbrace{\mathbb{E}\,V_{t+1}(\text{next state})}_{\text{best you can do from tomorrow}}\Big]$$ The best total from here = the best choice of (today's payoff + the best total from wherever today's choice lands you).

Vocabulary

State \(x\)Where you are now: the animal's weight, units on the shelf, your belief about a campaign.
Control \(u\)What you decide now: feed, units to order, which campaign to run.
ObjectiveThe scorecard you maximize (profit) or minimize (loss = −profit).
Discount factor \(\delta\)A dollar next period is worth \(\delta<1\) dollars today (\(\delta = e^{-r}\)).
PolicyA rule giving the best decision for every possible state.
Value function \(V(x)\)The best total you can still earn starting from state \(x\).

1 · Deterministic control: how much to feed a steer

A feedlot buys a steer and sells it after \(T\) weeks. More feed means a heavier animal and more revenue at sale — but feed costs money now. The computer works backwards from the sale date to find the best feed every week.

The problem (from class)
$$\dot w = h(w,f)\qquad\quad \max_{f(\cdot)}\; e^{-rT}\,p\,w(T)\;-\;c\int_0^T f(t)\,e^{-rt}\,dt$$ \(\dot w\) = how fast weight changes; \(h\) = the growth rule. Profit = discounted sale revenue − discounted feed bill.
Closed-form answer for Model A (\(h=a\sqrt f\)) — the code checks against it
$$f^*_t=\Big(\frac{a\,p}{2c}\Big)^2\,\delta^{\,2(T-t)}$$ Model B has no formula — only the computer can solve it.

Try this

  • Double the feed price \(c\) to 0.30. Feed should drop to about a quarter — the formula has \(c^2\) underneath.
  • Lower δ to 0.99 (about 52% a year). Feed now rises more steeply toward the sale date: early feed ties up money longer.
  • Switch to Model B. Feeding a heavy animal pays less, so the plan feeds much less.

The full ordering rule

The stochastic Bellman equation for the store
$$V(x)=\max_{y\ge x}\;\mathbb{E}_D\Big[\,p\min(y,D)-c\,(y-x)-h\,x'+\delta\,V(x')\Big],\qquad x'=y-\min(y,D)$$ \(x\) = units on hand, \(y\) = order up to, \(D\sim\text{Poisson}\). The only change from Topic 1: average over the randomness.
Rule of thumb that explains the safety stock
$$F_D(S)\;\ge\;\frac{\text{margin lost per unit short}}{\text{margin lost}+\text{cost per unit over}}=\frac{p-c}{(p-c)+h+(1-\delta)c}$$ Running out costs a lot more than holding one extra unit, so stock into the right tail of demand.

Try this

  • Raise the holding cost to 3. Storage is now expensive, so the safety stock shrinks.
  • Raise the selling price to 30. Running out hurts more, so the safety stock grows.
  • Change average demand to 20. Does the buffer stay at 4 units, or grow? (Poisson spread grows like \(\sqrt{\text{mean}}\).)

3a · Reinforcement learning: testing a new ad campaign

Each week the team runs either a proven campaign (known odds) or a new campaign that is either good or bad — nobody knows which. The state is the belief \(\phi\) = P(new campaign is good). Bayes' rule updates it; the Bellman equation decides when to experiment.

S = new campaign wonF = new campaign flopped1 = back to proven campaign

Belief that the new campaign is good

Learning step: Bayes' rule
$$\phi_{\text{win}}=\frac{\phi\,\alpha_2}{\phi\,\alpha_2+(1-\phi)\,\beta_2}\qquad\qquad \phi_{\text{flop}}=\frac{\phi\,(1-\alpha_2)}{\phi\,(1-\alpha_2)+(1-\phi)(1-\beta_2)}$$
Control step: Bellman with a belief as the state
$$V^*(\phi)=\max\Big\{\underbrace{(1-\delta)\,r_1+\delta V^*(\phi)}_{\text{proven: belief unchanged}},\;\underbrace{(1-\delta)\,r_2(\phi)+\delta\big[P_{\text{win}}V^*(\phi_{\text{win}})+P_{\text{flop}}V^*(\phi_{\text{flop}})\big]}_{\text{new: earn and learn}}\Big\}$$

Try this

  • Lower \(\delta\) to 0.5. The future matters less, so learning is worth less: the optimal switch moves toward the myopic one.
  • Raise \(\delta\) to 0.99. A patient team experiments even at tiny beliefs.
  • Set the truth to "bad" and rerun a few times. How many weeks until the team gives up? Once it stops, it never returns — the proven campaign teaches nothing new.

Learned rule vs. the DP rule

The Q-learning update
$$Q(x,y)\;\leftarrow\;Q(x,y)+\alpha\Big[\underbrace{r+\delta\max_{y'}Q(x',y')}_{\text{what just happened + best guess of the future}}-Q(x,y)\Big]$$ The Bellman equation with the expectation replaced by one real outcome at a time.

Try this

  • Set exploration to 0. The learner locks onto its first guesses and can end with a worse rule.
  • Use only 100,000 weeks. The rule is rougher — the price of not knowing the model is data.
  • Look at the "% of weeks" column: the rule is accurate where the store spends time and rough where it rarely goes.
